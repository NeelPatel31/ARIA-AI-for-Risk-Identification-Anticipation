# News: Arabica Prices Spike as Brazilian Frost Threatens Harvest

- Event Type: Economic/Price Shock
- Location Affected: Minas Gerais, Brazil (with secondary effects on Vietnamese robusta supply)
- Material Affected: Coffee Beans
- Date: 2026-08-29
- Summary: Arabica coffee futures jumped 13% this week after an unseasonal frost swept through Minas Gerais, Brazil's largest growing region, damaging an estimated 8-10% of flowering trees ahead of the next harvest cycle. The National Coffee Council of Brazil said crews are still assessing the full extent of the damage, but early estimates suggest recovery could take two to three growing seasons for the hardest-hit farms. "We have not seen frost this severe at this altitude in over a decade," a council agronomist told Reuters. Roasters are reportedly shifting blend ratios toward Vietnamese robusta to offset expected arabica shortfalls, pushing robusta prices up in tandem. Analysts caution that any further cold snaps before the frost-damaged trees recover could extend the price rally well into next year. Retail coffee prices are expected to lag the futures move by several months as existing inventory works through the supply chain.
