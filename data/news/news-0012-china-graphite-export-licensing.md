# News: Beijing Tightens Export Licensing on Battery-Grade Graphite

- Event Type: Government Policy
- Location Affected: China (nationwide)
- Material Affected: Graphite
- Date: 2026-07-17
- Summary: China's Ministry of Commerce announced Thursday that exporters of battery-grade graphite and related anode materials must obtain individual shipment licenses starting next month, expanding a framework first introduced for other battery minerals. The General Administration of Customs will require exporters to declare end-use and destination country for each shipment, a process trade groups say could add 2-3 weeks to export processing times. "This is about visibility into where strategic battery materials are ultimately going," a ministry spokesperson said at a regular briefing, without naming specific countries of concern. The policy applies nationwide and covers both natural and synthetic graphite grades used in battery anodes. Industry analysts note the move mirrors earlier restrictions on gallium and germanium, and expect downstream battery manufacturers outside China to begin qualifying alternative graphite sources as a hedge. Domestic battery makers are reportedly exempt from the licensing requirement for material consumed within China.
- Estimated Impact: High — nationwide licensing delay affecting all Graphite exports regardless of originating province
